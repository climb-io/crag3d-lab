# Shooting

繁體中文：[SHOOTING.md](SHOOTING.md)

Shoot **two sets** for a crag: close-up (builds the near wall) and step-back (so readers standing on the trail see the whole wall without fog). The illustrated guide is Climbio's [How to shoot 3D photos](https://github.com/climb-io/climbio/blob/main/en/guides/shoot-3d.html) page (the crag 3D section); this is the short version.

## Why two sets

A Gaussian splat is only sharp near where photos were taken. Readers in Redpoint look at the whole wall from the trail; if no photo was taken from there, that view is guessed by the model and comes out foggy with ghosting at the top. We retrained twice from the 790 close-up photos and the step-back views were worse than the original model both times.

## Step-back set

1. Stand on the trail about 8–12 m from the wall (far enough that top and bottom are both in frame). Do not move closer or climb onto loose rock.
2. Walk an arc along the wall, one photo every 1–2 m, facing the wall in each.
3. One pass each at high, mid and low (arms raised looking down at the upper wall, chest height, crouched for the base and the ground).
4. Aim up at the top, the canopy and the skyline; walk about 5 m past each edge of the wall and shoot back.
5. At inside corners, arêtes and bends, shoot another set about 35° to the left and to the right as well as facing the wall.

The close-up rules still apply: 1× main lens, no digital zoom, keep original photos with GPS, even light, avoid people, ropes and gear, a calm day (trees and grass blur when they sway). Count follows the spacing: a 20 m wall is a few dozen photos; how many are actually needed is still being tested.

## Close-up set

Station method: stand at one point, shoot up, down and all the way round, then walk to the next; keep 70–80% overlap between neighbouring photos; move one way along a safe route and do not backtrack.
